Curriculum

The same path students see in the Learning Portal.

The Learning Portal holds the final version of the curriculum, and this page mirrors it. Seven sections: three foundation sections that are fully available today, then four lab levels — Level 1 is complete, and Levels 2–4 are the planned path to around 100 labs. Every lab has starter code, step-by-step instructions, and a debug checklist, and runs on real hardware.

Two tracks, one path · Planned

A Middle School track and a High School track.

Two curriculums at different difficulty levels, with overlapping features and skills, that all connect together at the end.

Middle School track
Lower difficulty, same robot.

The same demos, joints, and ROS 2 ideas, paced for younger students — still real code moving real hardware.

Overlapping features and skills

Both tracks use the same Learning Portal, the same pre-installed demos, the same joint commands and safety rules, and the same four levels — Control, React, Perceive, Create.

It all connects at the end

The two tracks converge at the end of the path, in Level 4 — Create, where every student builds toward the same final showcase.

High School track
Higher difficulty, deeper systems.

The same skills pushed further — more original code and more of the perception, state-machine, and multi-node work that leads into the project track.

The path in the Learning Portal today is the foundation both tracks are built from. Track-specific lab versions are planned and will be released as development continues.

Instructor support

Students learn with zero prior experience — no robotics or engineering teacher required.

The Learning Portal teaches students directly, step by step, through setup, programming, testing, debugging, safety, and repair — no one on staff needs a robotics or engineering background to bring S1 into a classroom. Basic computer familiarity is recommended. This removes the need for specialized instructor expertise; it does not remove the need for an adult to supervise the classroom.

Real-robot wait times depend on instructor approval, queue length, and run duration. Each robot is designed for two authorized administrator accounts, so approval can be shared across teachers or a club advisor.

Purchasing

Pricing, pilots, and how many robots to buy.

Price

S1 is $6,999.99 per unit. Replacement parts typically cost $100 or less per limb, so accidental damage during classroom use stays cheap to fix. The cloud subscription is $12 per month or $100 per year, with the first year free on every purchase. Contact our sales team for pilot availability, package details, and institutional terms.

Payment & purchasing methods

No hidden fees — the price you're quoted is the price you pay. Purchase order, invoicing, and payment method details will be confirmed before commercial release.

How many robots does a program need?

Size the robot fleet around supervised hardware time, team rotation, and class length.

Robotics club or STEM team: one robot, teams of up to 15 students rotating engineering roles.
Single class section (middle or high school): one robot per rotating team of students, scheduled across the class period.
Multi-section CTE or engineering program: multiple robots shared across sections; future multi-robot features are planned to support coordinated, team-based labs.
