What's included

One robot package.

🤖
SwayForm Robot Unit

21-DOF humanoid platform, assembled and tested before shipping. Raspberry Pi 5 (8 GB) pre-configured with Linux, ROS 2, and the SwayForm software stack.

📘
Free Learning Portal Access

Core documentation, setup guides, safety notes, repair tutorials, and the full curriculum path — 14 readings, 5 demos, and the 10 Level 1 labs available today — free, with no license required.

☁️
Cloud Subscription

Hosted classroom accounts, the code queue that sends student programs to the robot, live video, and simulation. Three plans: Classroom Hosting (no simulation), Standard (3 concurrent simulation environments) and Premium (15 concurrent, no wait) — see Access & plans below.

🔧
Replacement Parts Access

Every individual 3D-printed replacement part is priced below $20. Schools may also download available print files and produce structural parts themselves. Electronic and mechanical replacement costs vary by component — see Repair.

Setup requirements
✓
Power outlet — standard wall power feeding the included 12V/50A supply. S1 has no internal battery and runs plugged in at all times, so there's no charging downtime or battery to replace.
✓
School Wi-Fi — S1 joins your existing Wi-Fi like any other device, a simple one-time setup. Students reach it through the browser-based Learning Portal — no SSH or laptop-to-robot setup.
✓
Student computers — any computer with a modern web browser. Students write and run code in the Learning Portal's built-in editor, so there is nothing to install.
✓
Clearance on all sides when powered — tabletop or a dedicated cart. No mounting required.

Curriculum

The same path students see in the Learning Portal.

The Learning Portal holds the final version of the curriculum, and this page mirrors it. Seven sections: three foundation sections that are fully available today, then four lab levels — Level 1 is complete, and Levels 2–4 are the planned path to around 100 labs. Two tracks, one path · Planned

A Middle School track and a High School track.

Two curriculums at different difficulty levels, with overlapping features and skills, that all connect together at the end.

Middle School track
Lower difficulty, same robot.

The same demos, joints, and ROS 2 ideas, paced for younger students — still real code moving real hardware.

Overlapping features and skills

Both tracks use the same Learning Portal, the same pre-installed demos, the same joint commands and safety rules, and the same four levels — Control, React, Perceive, Create.

It all connects at the end

The two tracks converge at the end of the path, in Level 4 — Create, where every student builds toward the same final showcase.

High School track
Higher difficulty, deeper systems.

The same skills pushed further — more original code and more of the perception, state-machine, and multi-node work that leads into the project track.

The path in the Learning Portal today is the foundation both tracks are built from. Track-specific lab versions are planned and will be released as development continues.

Instructor support

Students learn with zero prior experience — no robotics or engineering teacher required.

The Learning Portal teaches students directly, step by step, through setup, programming, testing, debugging, safety, and repair — no one on staff needs a robotics or engineering background to bring S1 into a classroom. Basic computer familiarity is recommended. This removes the need for specialized instructor expertise; it does not remove the need for an adult to supervise the classroom.

Purchasing

Pricing, pilots, and how many robots to buy.

Price

S1 is estimated at $5,000–$8,000 per unit. Replacement parts typically cost $100 or less per limb, so accidental damage during classroom use stays cheap to fix. Cloud plans are priced separately, and that pricing is being finalized. Contact our sales team for pilot availability, package details, and final institutional pricing.

Availability

Pilot interest is currently open. Commercial availability is targeted for Fall 2027.

Payment & purchasing methods

No hidden fees — the price you're quoted is the price you pay. Purchase order, invoicing, and payment method details will be confirmed before commercial release.

How many robots does a program need?

Standard offers 3 concurrent simulation environments per robot; Premium offers 15 concurrent environments. Classroom Hosting has no simulation. Size the robot fleet around supervised hardware time, team rotation, and class length.

Robotics club or STEM team: one robot, teams of up to 15 students rotating engineering roles.
Single class section (middle or high school): one robot per rotating team of students, scheduled across the class period.
Multi-section CTE or engineering program: multiple robots shared across sections; future multi-robot features are planned to support coordinated, team-based labs.
