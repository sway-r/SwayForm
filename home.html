Safety and instructor control

Every command is validated, queued, and approved.

Student programs never talk to the motors directly. A protected motion-control node is the only software layer permitted to send final commands — enforcing joint limits, speed limits, and command timeouts on all 21 degrees of freedom.

01

Layered physical safety

A normal software stop is always available, and a physical emergency stop / servo-power cutoff is being validated ahead of classroom release — see Safety.

02

Instructor execution control

Instructors can view every queued submission, review the source, approve or reject it, and start or stop any running program at any time.

03

Open development, protected motion

Students get real, open ROS 2 development. The robot itself only ever executes what's been validated and approved — not unrestricted motor access.

What I cannot create, I do not understand.

Students understand robotics on a deeper level when their code moves real hardware. Rather than only observing concepts on a screen, students must transform their designs into a working system — revealing both the strengths and limitations of their understanding.

Repairability

Built to be fixed, not replaced.

SwayForm combines commercially available electronics and actuators with custom, replaceable 3D-printed structural parts. When something breaks, schools replace the affected part — not the whole robot.

01

Parts under $20

Every individual 3D-printed replacement part is priced below $20. Schools may also download the available print files and produce parts themselves.

02

Repair is a lesson, not a ticket

The Learning Hub includes component identification, diagnostics, disassembly, calibration, and safety guidance — many repairs are a supervised student learning opportunity.

03

No single point of failure

When a larger assembly is damaged, schools replace only the affected printed or electronic components. Electronic and mechanical replacement costs vary by component.

Common questions

Questions we get asked.

Who is SwayForm designed for?
High school engineering programs, community college robotics courses, student clubs, STEM labs, and any educational setting where students can work with real hardware regularly. It is not a consumer product.
Does one robot support a whole class?
Yes. One unit is designed for teams of up to 15 students using rotating engineering roles, or a classroom where groups take turns. Programs can add more units later when they are ready to scale.
What background do instructors need?
No prior ROS 2 or humanoid-robotics experience is required. Basic computer familiarity is recommended — the Learning Hub guides instructors and students through setup, programming, testing, debugging, safety, and repair.
Does it need internet access?
The robot's control, perception, ROS 2 environment, and physical execution all run locally on the onboard Raspberry Pi. Internet is used for things like opening the hosted Learning Hub or downloading updates, not for core robot operation.
What comes with the unit?
A robot with Linux, ROS 2, and five demo programs pre-installed, plus free, permanent access to the Learning Hub and six months of the premium subscription. Currently in pilot development — commercial availability targeted for Fall 2027.
Is SwayForm available to order now?
Pilot interest is currently open. Commercial availability is targeted for Fall 2027. Contact our sales team and we will keep you updated on timing.
Can the labs fit into an existing course?
Yes. Begin with the 10 available Level 1 labs, with the curriculum expanding to 40 across four levels. Programs can use selected labs as part of an existing syllabus or follow the full path in order.
What happens if a component breaks?
SwayForm combines commercially available electronics and actuators with custom, replaceable 3D-printed structural parts. Every individual printed part is priced below $20, and the Learning Hub includes a full repair guide.
