Curriculum

What students actually learn.

Concrete subject areas — not lab numbers. Designed for students, instructors, and program directors who need to know what working with SwayForm actually teaches.

01

ROS 2 Nodes and Topics

Students author, launch, and debug ROS 2 nodes from scratch. They understand publishers, subscribers, message types, QoS settings, and the node graph. By Level 2, every student has written a node that both publishes and subscribes at the same time.

rclpypub / subros2 topic echorqt_graph
02

Servo Control

Students trace the path from a Python float to physical joint movement — angle value, safe range clamping, driver write, servo response. They calibrate individual joints, observe the precision limits of PWM, and understand why voltage regulation matters for repeatable motion.

Joint targetsSafe rangesPWM timingMG996R / MG90S
03

Depth Camera Perception

Students work with the Intel D435i to extract both color and per-pixel depth data. They implement color segmentation in HSV space, face detection, and depth-based proximity triggers. They learn why camera minimum range matters and how lighting changes detection reliability.

D435iHSV segmentationHaar cascadeDepth reads
04

Sensor Feedback Loops

Students design systems where sensor readings drive actuator commands in real time. They encounter noise, latency, and the difference between a simple threshold and a hysteresis band. The IMU, ultrasonic sensor, and camera each introduce different failure modes worth studying.

IMUHC-SR04HysteresisFeedback control
05

Mechanical Limits

Students learn that hardware has hard constraints. They encounter joint stops, understand why range clamping matters before a command reaches hardware, and see what happens when it doesn't. This is not abstract — students who skip safe-range checks hear the servo strain.

Joint clampingSafe rangesDriver validationPhysical limits
06

Power Distribution

Students examine the two-rail power architecture: why the servo rail and Pi rail are separated, what happens to a Pi when servo current spikes reach it, and how to read voltage under load. Practical electrical reasoning, not just theory.

12V main railBuck convertersRail separationVoltage under load
07

Debugging Real Hardware

Students learn that hardware debugging is different from software debugging. They use i2cdetect, ros2 topic echo, rqt_graph, and a multimeter as diagnostic tools. They learn to isolate the question: is this a code bug, a wiring issue, or a configuration problem?

i2cdetectros2 topic echorqt_graphVoltage testing
08

Motion Sequencing

Students author multi-phase motion sequences: define keyframes, interpolate between them, manage timing, and compose sequences into reusable functions. They observe that 20 Hz is sufficient for smooth motion — and why publishing at 100 Hz changes nothing except CPU load.

KeyframesInterpolationLoop rateSequence composition
09

Human-Robot Interaction

Students design systems that respond to people — proximity detection, face tracking, gesture responses. They encounter the design tension between responsiveness and stability. Hysteresis, dead zones, and state machine guard conditions stop being theoretical the first time the robot twitches uncontrollably without them.

Proximity triggersFace trackingDead zonesState guards
